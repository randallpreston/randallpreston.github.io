Undergraduate Research Scholar Program March 2026–Present Ongoing

Hydrogen Permeation Testing in Structural Metals

ME³ Lab: Materials Engineering in Extreme Environments

In Villanova’s ME³ Lab, I work with Dr. Andrew Lee and a team of six undergraduate and doctoral researchers. We study how hydrogen moves through structural metals, which matters for energy and automotive systems that use hydrogen.

Advisor
Dr. Andrew Lee, Department of Mechanical Engineering
Timeline
March 2026–Present
Program
Undergraduate Research Scholar Program
Method
Electrochemical permeation (Devanathan–Stachurski)
Project Updates

01 / Context

Why hydrogen permeation matters

Hydrogen can enter structural metals, diffuse through them and degrade their mechanical properties, a problem known as hydrogen embrittlement. As energy and automotive systems take on more hydrogen, engineers need reliable data on how quickly it moves through the metals those systems are built from.

Permeation testing measures exactly that.

My role

  • Design, build and validate an electrochemical permeation test setup based on the Devanathan–Stachurski dual cell
  • Meet weekly with the team and Dr. Lee to keep the lab’s task forces aligned
  • Use results to help set parameters for other experiments across the lab

02 / Method

How the dual cell works

A thin metal specimen separates two electrochemical cells. Hydrogen is generated on one side, diffuses through the metal, and is measured as an electrical current on the other.
